# Tail of the Comet

![Pasted image 20260917181202.png](/img/user/_meta/_attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260917181202.png)

Contrary to what many believe, a comet's tail always points away from the Sun, since its the solar wind that creates the tail in the first place.  The motion of the comet itself rarely plays anything more than a very minor role in the shape of the tail.

The comet still has a few more weeks left of outgassing as it sheds the heat absorbed during its solar flyby.  Cometary surface operations are still largely shut down.  Traffic to and from the comet is still heavily restricted, especially so since the attempt by Venus to capture the heavy lift pad.

Our teams in the belt have been busy spreading the "comet zombie" rumors with the help of the empty morphs you acquired a few months ago.  

Tension between the major powers is at an all-time high, and Venus seems to be nearing another quiet civil war among its houses.

All combined, it means that we've been very successful in regulating exactly who and what goes to the comet, and what is allowed to be removed from the comet.  Firewall is largely convinced that no cometary material samples of any significance have been removed for study.  

One thing we can't police very well is the cometary tail.  No surprise there, its nearly 40 million km long.  Normally this shouldn't be a problem since the density of the tail is so low that any normal methods of sampling wouldn't yield enough monopole material to be worth study.

Monolith has chosen to deploy a non-standard sampling method

# Magnetic Scoop

Much of the material in the cometary tail is ionized.  This make it easy to manipulate with magnetic fields.  Worse, we suspect a magnetic scoop, designed and tuned properly, will capture a higher-than-normal quality of monopole material.

A Monolith transport (*MV Redhorn*) left Mercury on June 10th, bound for Saturn.  This transport is part of the normal scheduling cycle of their commercial fleet so it didn't raise any flags on its own.  

*Redhorn* passed directly through the cometary tail.  Again this on its own didn't raise any concerns.  The vessel was a few hundred thousand kilometers from the comet itself, too far to trigger any traffic control oversight.  The distance the comet is at now from the Sun means the density of the tail itself is no real danger to a modern ship so long as relative speeds are low.  Since the transport is flying out to Saturn, its speed relative to the comet's tail is low.  

With no danger to the ship, maintaining its standard schedule, and well beyond the regulated space around the comet, no one care to look any further.

##### Tethys

If you recall, we used Monolith's ice mining station on Tethys as part of the cover story during the heist of *Nyx Paradox*.  More recently, we've had teams out there trying to find the best ways to quietly gather the ice we will need to get Skamander's biome functional. 

We got lucky.  A team on Tethys stumbled into some intel that didn't quite make sense until *Redhorn* made its pass through the tail.

It seems a small team of Monolith's *Special Project Division* is on Tethys, waiting for the arrival of what they call *The Dirty Snowball*.  Their job is to package this "snowball" in with normal ice mined from Tethys, for return to Mercury.  Hidden among kilotons of normal ice will likely make it impossible to find this "snowball".

Observation of the region of the cometary tail after the *Redhorn* passed through it suggests they deployed a very large magnetic scoop, likely upward of several hundred kilometers wide.  A scoop that large could have collected several kilograms of comet material and, as stated above, an unusually high ratio of monopoles.
