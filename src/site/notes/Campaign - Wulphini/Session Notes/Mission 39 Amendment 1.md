# Things you know

###### Ice Mining
Most of the large-scale orbital operations for mining frozen volatiles have been suspended for the last year.  The equipment and manpower moved to the inner system to support comet operations.

This has been a major boon for Tethys City, which has become one of the few places to buy ice in bulk, and part of why this station is growing rapidly.

It also means that Tethys City has a wide range of visitors from several nations and many cultures.  Your odd little group doesn't stand out at all here.

Due to the rapid shift in market forces, large buyers are getting priority access to sales teams and exports while small parties are stuck waiting.

###### Rapid Expansion
Tethys City, as explained above, has expanded faster than designed.  This doesn't seem to have strained the resources of Monolith.  In fact, it's pretty clear Monolith is using the boom to harvest visitor data, using it for predatory sales tactics.

You've only been here a single day, and yet you're all already aware that the Station Manager will likely notice deviations to your habits here.

They likely know:

- Serenity has ties to the Eden Collective, and probably assumes she can rely on untraceable lines of communication.
- Lou has expensive desires but is too frugal to impulse buy.  Given his background and education, it's very likely your team will be assigned an exceptionally well-trained sales team when you make your appointment.  They might also realize that Lou could be in a position to engage a successful legal challenge to Monolith on the heavy-handed surveillance here, at least in the GovCentral-registered Tethys City.  
- Cobalt is far more comfortable on his own ship, somehow managed to bypass Monolith Utilities in refueling Silent Eternity, and has several annoying regulatory exemptions.  Given the armor and temporarily confiscated heavy weapons, its likely Cobalt has elevated surveillance on him, though Monolith probably won't risk a political incident by being loud about that fact.
- Bromar is a Monolith employee that is using employee perks but has not (yet?) announced the official purpose of his unscheduled arrival.  Monolith-Tethys HQ here is likely already familiarizing themselves with the reports Bromar's private mining team has generated this year.

#  Investigation Results: Engineers

The engineering team is composed of four technicians, two pilots, two mech operators, one safety officer, one lead engineer, along with two zero-g construction mechs, 12 service proxies, 1 light observation/rescue shuttle, and 1 heavy lift transport shuttle.

A team of ten equipped with several million dollars worth of gear and 2 Monolith-flagged vessels might be difficult to "impersonate", but that's what it is.

Judging by the teams collective qualification, the gear being issued, and the timeline of their operation plan, you conclude they will be removing several tons of bulky equipment from the *Redhorn*.  This aligns with what you know of magnetic scoops.  

Something unexpected was uncovered while researching the type of scoop that could have been fitted to the *Redhorn*.  You've learned that a scoop of this type can't be entirely shutdown.  The long, physical booms that act as the initial wave-guides for the magnetic net they cast, require a minimum level of power to maintain their shape.  In effect, each of the hundred-plus meter boom arms will be energized even during disassembly.  Without this idle power, the booms will resonate with Saturn's own magnetic field and could quickly tangle themselves into a knotted mess.

To make things worse, Saturn's magnetosphere is rich with plasma thanks to the ice volcanos of Enceladus.  This isn't an issue for normal traffic in the area, but the scoop will be at idle power.  That's enough to ensure a steady intake of local plasma.  The collection device at the bottom of the scoop will be venting this idle-capture plasma like a massive blowtorch. A torch without an off-button.  There is a reason a highly specialized team of qualified techs has been chosen to do this.
